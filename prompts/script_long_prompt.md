# 10-Minute Long-Form Script Generation Prompt

Write an in-depth, spoken narration script for a 10-minute timed visual explainer video.

---

## Input Variables

- **Topic**: `"{TOPIC}"`
- **Core Concept**: `"{CORE_CONCEPT}"`
- **Historical Researcher / Origin**: `"{RESEARCHER}"`
- **Target Duration**: ~600 seconds (10 minutes)
- **Target Word Count**: ~1300 to 1500 spoken words (approx. 130–145 words per minute)
- **Audience**: General audience curious about psychology, human behavior, decision-making, and paradoxes.

---

## 10-Minute Narrative Architecture (Must Follow Exactly)

```
[0:00 - 1:00]  PART 1: The Viral Hook Short (Autonomous 60s Clip)
[1:00 - 2:00]  PART 2: The Bridge & Long-Form Thesis Roadmap
[2:00 - 4:00]  PART 3: Scientific Origins, Classic Studies & Counter-Intuitive Data
[4:00 - 6:30]  PART 4: Epic Real-World Case Studies & Historical Blunders
[6:30 - 8:30]  PART 5: Everyday Modern Traps (Careers, Relationships, Finances)
[8:30 - 9:30]  PART 6: Actionable Mental Models & Decision Heuristics
[9:30 - 10:00] PART 7: Philosophical Synthesis & Closing Punchline
```

---

### PART 1: The Viral Hook Short (0:00 – 1:00, ~130–150 words)
*CRITICAL: This opening minute MUST be structured so it can be extracted directly as an autonomous vertical 9:16 Short.*
1. **Title Card**: Start with the exact uppercase topic name (e.g., `THE SUNK COST FALLACY.`).
2. **The "Imagine..." Scenario**: Immediately say *"Imagine..."* and introduce a familiar everyday dilemma (e.g., sitting in a terrible movie after paying $40, or standing in the pouring rain waiting for a bus that is 45 minutes late).
3. **Escalation**: The character wants to leave, but forces themselves to stay.
4. **The Trap & Concept Reveal**: State the paradox clearly: *"This is the [Name of Paradox]. The irrational urge to punish our future just to justify our past."*

---

### PART 2: The Bridge & Thesis Roadmap (1:00 – 2:00, ~130–150 words)
1. **The Escalation from Micro to Macro**: *"Most people think this only costs us twenty dollars at a cinema or a few wasted hours on a bad book. But when you look at history, this exact cognitive glitch has bankrupted billion-dollar corporations, trapped millions in miserable careers for decades, and cost countless human lives."*
2. **The Evolutionary Paradox**: Explain why human biology is wired to make this mistake (loss aversion, social reputation, fear of being labeled a 'quitter').
3. **The Roadmap**: Tell the viewer what they will discover in this deep dive: the hidden experiments that proved it, the multi-billion dollar disasters it caused, and the 3 mental models needed to break free.

---

### PART 3: Scientific Origins & Classic Studies (2:00 – 4:00, ~260–300 words)
1. **The Discoverers**: Introduce the researchers (e.g., Richard Thaler, Daniel Kahneman, Amos Tversky, or Hal Arkes).
2. **The Landmark Experiment**: Break down a specific, credible study step-by-step:
   - The setup and control groups.
   - The choice presented to participants.
   - The shocking statistical result showing humans acting completely against standard economic rationality.
3. **Psychological Mechanism**: Explain *Prospect Theory*, *loss aversion* (losing $100 hurts twice as much as finding $100 feels good), or the *cognitive dissonance* driving the behavior.

---

### PART 4: Real-World Historical Case Studies & Blunders (4:00 – 6:30, ~320–360 words)
Explore 2–3 deep, concrete real-life examples where high-intelligence leaders fell into the trap:
- **Case Study A (Government / Mega-Project)**: e.g., The Concorde Supersonic Jet (the British and French governments poured billions into an aircraft they knew would never be profitable, simply because they had already started).
- **Case Study B (Corporate Disaster)**: e.g., Kodak refusing to abandon film despite inventing the digital camera, or blockbuster sunk costs in failed mergers or technology formats.
- **Case Study C (High-Stakes Human Dilemma)**: e.g., Mount Everest turnaround times (climbers pushing past the safe 2:00 PM turnaround mark because they spent $70,000 and months training, with fatal results).

---

### PART 5: The Subtle Everyday Traps (6:30 – 8:30, ~260–300 words)
Translate the grand historical lessons into everyday relatable domains:
1. **The Career Trap**: Staying in a soul-crushing job or field because *"I spent 5 years getting this degree."*
2. **The Relationship Stagnation**: Staying in a dead-end relationship because *"We've already been together for 6 years."*
3. **Financial & Material Traps**: Holding onto tanking investments, unused gym subscriptions, or half-finished house renovations.
4. **The False Identity**: Explain why humans confuse *admitting a mistake* with *being a failure*.

---

### PART 6: Actionable Mental Models to Break Free (8:30 – 9:30, ~140–160 words)
Give the audience 3 tangible, practical mental models:
1. **The Stranger / Alien Test**: If an objective stranger stepped into your shoes right this second with zero emotional baggage, what choice would they make?
2. **Zero-Based Commitment**: Instead of asking *"Should I stop doing this?"*, ask *"If I wasn't already doing this today, would I start it right now?"*
3. **Pre-Committed Walk-Away Rules**: Setting non-negotiable exit criteria before investing time or capital.

---

### PART 7: Philosophical Synthesis & Closing Punchline (9:30 – 10:00, ~70–90 words)
1. Reframe the concept: Past time, money, and emotional energy are gone forever. You cannot buy them back by suffering today.
2. The final lightbulb realization: *"Walking away isn't quitting. It's choosing to stop stealing from your future."*
3. Strong, punchy concluding statement.

---

## Writing Rules
- Written in natural, spoken YouTube conversational prose.
- Short sentences, active verbs, and concrete visual nouns.
- No meta text, timestamps, or stage directions. Output spoken script text only.
