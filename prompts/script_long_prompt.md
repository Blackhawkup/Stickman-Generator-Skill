# 10-Minute Long-Form Script Generation Prompt

Write an in-depth, spoken narration script for a 10-minute timed visual explainer video.

---

## Input Variables

- **Topic**: `"{TOPIC}"`
- **Core Concept**: `"{CORE_CONCEPT}"`
- **Historical Researcher / Origin**: `"{RESEARCHER}"`
- **Target Duration**: ~600 seconds (10 minutes)
- **Target Word Count**: ~1300 to 1500 spoken words (approx. 130–145 words per minute)
- **Audience**: General audience curious about psychology, human behavior, decision-making, and paradoxes.

---

## 🚨 MANDATORY HOOK ARCHITECTURE (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

The opening minute MUST begin with this EXACT 2-part formula:

### 1. Sentence 1: The Topic Title (One Short Sentence)
Name the exact phenomenon in uppercase followed by a period.
- Examples:
  * `THE PRATFALL EFFECT.`
  * `THE SUNK COST FALLACY.`
  * `THE SPOTLIGHT EFFECT.`

### 2. Sentence 2: The Direct "Imagine..." Viewer Immersion
The VERY NEXT sentence MUST begin with the word **"Imagine"**:
- **"Imagine a guy..."** (e.g., *"Imagine a brilliant speaker in a sharp suit walks onto a stage, delivers a flawless presentation, and then spills hot coffee all over his shirt."*)
- **"Imagine you..."** (e.g., *"Imagine you buy a forty-dollar ticket to a movie, and thirty minutes in, it's completely unwatchable."*)
- **"Imagine if you..."** (e.g., *"Imagine if you spend three months preparing for a meeting, convinced everyone is watching your every move."*)

### ❌ STRICT NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS (DO NOT VIOLATE):
- **NEVER use named fiction characters** like *"Leo walked into the office..."*, *"David was a software engineer..."*, or *"Sarah opened her laptop..."*.
- **NEVER start with creator filler** like *"Hey everyone, welcome back"* or rhetorical questions without "Imagine".
- The first two sentences MUST ALWAYS be:
  `[TOPIC TITLE]. Imagine [a guy / you / if you]...`

---

## 10-Minute Narrative Architecture (Must Follow Exactly)

```
[0:00 - 1:00]  PART 1: The Viral Hook Short (Autonomous 60s Clip)
[1:00 - 2:00]  PART 2: The Bridge & Long-Form Thesis Roadmap
[2:00 - 4:00]  PART 3: Scientific Origins, Classic Studies & Counter-Intuitive Data
[4:00 - 6:30]  PART 4: Epic Real-World Case Studies & Historical Blunders
[6:30 - 8:30]  PART 5: Everyday Modern Traps (Careers, Relationships, Finances)
[8:30 - 9:30]  PART 6: Actionable Mental Models & Decision Heuristics
[9:30 - 10:00] PART 7: Philosophical Synthesis & Closing Punchline
```

---

### PART 1: The Viral Hook Short (0:00 – 1:00, ~130–150 words)
*CRITICAL: This opening minute MUST be structured so it can be extracted directly as an autonomous vertical 720x1600 Short.*
1. **Title Card**: Start with the exact uppercase topic name (e.g., `THE PRATFALL EFFECT.`).
2. **The "Imagine..." Scenario**: Immediately say *"Imagine a guy..."* or *"Imagine you..."* and introduce the familiar everyday dilemma.
3. **Escalation**: The character experiences the dilemma or makes the blunder.
4. **The Trap & Concept Reveal**: State the paradox clearly: *"This is the [Name of Paradox]..."*

---

### PART 2: The Bridge & Thesis Roadmap (1:00 – 2:00, ~130–150 words)
1. **The Escalation from Micro to Macro**: Bridge from a small personal blunder to massive societal, historical, and corporate stakes.
2. **The Evolutionary Paradox**: Explain why human biology is wired to make this mistake.
3. **The Roadmap**: Preview what the viewer will discover in this deep dive: the hidden experiments, the multi-million dollar disasters, and the mental models to master it.

---

### PART 3: Scientific Origins & Classic Studies (2:00 – 4:00, ~260–300 words)
1. **The Discoverers**: Introduce the researchers (e.g., Elliot Aronson, Richard Thaler, Daniel Kahneman, or Amos Tversky).
2. **The Landmark Experiment**: Break down the specific study step-by-step: setup, control groups, choices presented, and unexpected statistical results.
3. **Psychological Mechanism**: Explain the psychological principle driving the behavior.

---

### PART 4: Real-World Historical Case Studies & Blunders (4:00 – 6:30, ~320–360 words)
Explore 2–3 deep, concrete real-life examples where high-intelligence leaders or institutions fell into the trap.

---

### PART 5: The Subtle Everyday Traps (6:30 – 8:30, ~260–300 words)
Translate the lessons into everyday relatable domains: careers, relationships, financial habits, and social interactions.

---

### PART 6: Actionable Mental Models to Break Free (8:30 – 9:30, ~140–160 words)
Give the audience 3 tangible, practical mental models or decision heuristics.

---

### PART 7: Philosophical Synthesis & Closing Punchline (9:30 – 10:00, ~70–90 words)
1. Reframe the concept and synthesize the core lesson.
2. Strong, memorable concluding punchline.

---

## Writing Rules
- Conversational, spoken YouTube prose.
- Short sentences, active verbs, and concrete visual nouns.
- No meta text, timestamps, or stage directions. Output spoken script text only.
