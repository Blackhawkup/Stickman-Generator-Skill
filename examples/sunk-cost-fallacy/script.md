The Sunk Cost Fallacy.

Leo spends forty dollars on a VIP cinema ticket.

Twenty minutes in, the movie is completely unwatchable.

He is bored, miserable, and checking his watch every thirty seconds.

An exit door is right next to him.

Outside, the sun is shining and his friends are grabbing coffee.

Yet Leo forces himself to sit in the dark for two more torturous hours.

Why? Because he already paid for the ticket.

This psychological trap is the Sunk Cost Fallacy.

Coined by Nobel laureate economist Richard Thaler, it is our irrational urge to stick with a bad choice once we have invested money, time, or effort.

In a famous study, researchers gave theatergoers identical season tickets at different prices.

The people who paid full price forced themselves to attend far more miserable plays than those who got discounts, just to avoid feeling wasteful.

Our brains hate admitting a loss so much that we punish ourselves in the future.

We stay in soul-crushing jobs because of a past degree.

We stay in bad relationships because we invested three years.

And we finish awful meals just to clean our plates.

Here is the hard mathematical reality: the past is gone.

That forty dollars was spent whether you leave or stay.

Staying in the theater does not get your money back. It only steals two hours of your life.

Never sacrifice your future just to defend your past.

Walk out of the movie.
